## Claims we will use

- Sparse Krylov methods avoid a full eigensystem. Warm starts accelerate nearby ground-state calculations, but can suppress overlaps with excited states needed for gaps. Mix previous eigenstates and retain independent starts when necessary (Sec. 4.2, pp. 83–97).
- Energy convergence is insufficient: other observables can converge more slowly and nonmonotonically. Loss of Lanczos orthogonality produces spurious duplicate eigenvalues; reorthogonalization addresses it at a memory cost (Figs. 30–31).
- Symmetry blocks must use actual Hamiltonian symmetries. Translation, reflection and spin inversion are distinct. Reflection and momentum can be simultaneously treated in the simple form only at special momenta. The Heisenberg examples conserve total magnetization and have SU(2); our transverse-field ANNNI Hamiltonian does not share those symmetries (Sec. 4.1).
- Finite-size pseudocritical shifts depend on the observable and scaling corrections. Crossings can approach the same critical point from opposite directions. The review's numerical examples are not quantitative bounds for ANNNI rings with 8–12 sites (Secs. 3.3–3.6 and 5.3).
- Apparent power laws over short distance ranges can hide logarithmic corrections. Even Heisenberg chains of thousands of sites require care to resolve them (Sec. 5.3.1). This strengthens the case against claiming a floating phase from a few small-ring correlation points.

## Equations and conventions

The Krylov space is span{|v⟩, H|v⟩, …}; the normalized recurrence is βₘ₊₁vₘ₊₁ = Hvₘ − αₘvₘ − βₘvₘ₋₁. It assumes an orthonormal basis; numerical loss of that property must be checked.

Eq. (68), p. 37: S(q)=Σᵣ exp(−iqr)C(r). For translation-averaged C(r), this equals the 1/N-normalized double sum over sites. Consequently squared order is S(Q)/N. The spin-1/2 examples use Sᶻ=Z/2, so their longitudinal spin structure factors are one quarter of the Pauli-Z convention used here. Use the general definition rather than blindly copying specialized even-chain endpoint expressions in later examples.

Conventional scaling uses a variable (g−g_c)L^(1/ν), with observable-dependent corrections; the critical-gap scale is L^(−z). KT scaling instead involves an essential divergence ξ∼exp[b/√|g−g_c|], giving logarithmically slow drift of order 1/(ln L)² under ξ∼L. These are asymptotic forms, not a trustworthy fit with several unconstrained parameters to only N=8,10,12.

The QMC sections distinguish thermal susceptibility (an imaginary-time Kubo integral) from an equal-time structure factor. A classical variance formula for susceptibility applies directly only for conserved operators; it is not a generic replacement for ANNNI response functions.

## Parameters and numerical examples

The Lanczos illustrations include a 16-site Heisenberg chain with roughly 30 vectors sufficient for its ground-state energy to ten decimal places, versus about 60 for several low levels. Those are examples, not convergence thresholds for this challenge. The 32-site correlation example has a much shorter range of bulk-converged correlations than the full ring length. The subsequent QMC chain examples reach N=1024 and 4096 and explicitly verify temperature convergence.

For future ED, record eigenpair residuals, orthogonality, observable convergence and sector labels; compare small instances against dense results. If block diagonalizing, verify that the block dimensions sum to the full Hilbert-space dimension. These are applications of the review's validation advice, not checks already run in this literature step.

## Limitations

Single-vector Lanczos cannot recover an entire exactly degenerate eigenspace. Warm-started ground states alone are poor seeds for excited-state searches. Heisenberg singlet–triplet level-crossing recipes, numerical critical couplings, winding-number estimators and stiffness normalizations must not be transplanted into ANNNI unchanged.

The frustration-related QMC sign problem discussed for off-diagonal antiferromagnetic Heisenberg terms does not imply that this ANNNI Hamiltonian has the same problem: its frustration is diagonal in the Z basis and its transverse-field off-diagonal elements are nonpositive for h>0. The review explicitly distinguishes diagonal from off-diagonal frustration (p. 144).

No validated runtime guarantee, ANNNI-specific finite-size offset or noisy-circuit boundary prediction follows from this review. Its historical statements about the state of research are not current surveys.
