# [S19] Zero-noise extrapolation and cancellation

**Citation:** K. Temme, S. Bravyi and J. M. Gambetta, “Error mitigation for short-depth quantum circuits,” Phys. Rev. Lett. 119, 180509 (2017), doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.119.180509; arXiv:1612.02058. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.02058).

**Access/read status:** FULL. Full15-page preprint including supplement.

## Claims we will use

Richardson extrapolation cancels low-order terms in noise strength; probabilistic error cancellation uses a calibrated noise inverse represented by signed operations.

## Equations and conventions

E(λ)=E0+Σakλ^k. Choose Σγj=1 and Σγj cj^k=0 for k=1..m, then estimate E0≈ΣγjE(cjλ). Signed weights amplify variance; PEC overhead scales with squared quasiprobability norm.

## Parameters

Choose controlled scales with identical ideal circuit. Replacement-noise ε differs from PennyLane total-Pauli p: ε=4p/3.

## Limitations and unresolved points

Finite-order residual bias, calibration dependence and sampling cost remain. Neither scheme fixes a poor noiseless ansatz, an incorrect observable or finite-size error.

## Rubric relevance

Noise depth, sophistication.
