# Presentation notes (about 6.5 minutes, 9 slides)

Slides: `writeup/slides.pdf`. Every number below also appears in the report and in `team/results/analysis/`.

**1. Title (30 s).** We mapped the ANNNI phase diagram on small periodic rings, then asked what the challenge's noise model (a depolarizing channel on the target after every CNOT) does to it. Headline: at p=0.01 the ferromagnet disappears under fixed thresholds, even though the boundary positions defined by derivatives barely move. At p=0.05 the state is essentially maximally mixed. Zero-noise extrapolation restores the p=0.01 diagram.

**2. Model and diagnostics (45 s).** Three competing terms: ferromagnetic nearest-neighbour coupling, frustrating next-nearest-neighbour coupling, and a transverse field. Finite rings keep the global spin-flip symmetry, so ⟨Z⟩ vanishes even in ordered phases. We therefore use the structure factor at q=0 and q=π/2, with the uncorrelated floor subtracted, together with m_x. The overlay curves are approximate thermodynamic fits: the lower one is a Pokrovsky–Talapov transition and the upper one KT. They are references, not labels, and our thresholds come from limiting-case anchor regions rather than from these curves.

**3. Method and validation (45 s).** The reference is sparse exact diagonalisation; the circuit is a Hamiltonian-variational ansatz. We first tried a hardware-efficient ansatz, which could not get below 1–3 % energy error near the boundaries. The HVA reaches a median energy error of 4e-5 and agrees with the exact labels at 98.9 % of points. The cost is 128 CNOTs, so 128 noise channels. Every layer of the pipeline is validated against PennyLane to about 1e-14.

**4. Clean diagram and finite size (60 s).** The ferro boundary hardly depends on N and sits on the Ising estimate; the fidelity-susceptibility peak moves towards the DMRG value. The antiphase boundary drifts down strongly with N towards the KT line, which is the expected slow, KT-like finite-size behaviour. N=10 cannot hold a period-4 pattern and is anomalous. On the floating phase we are careful: at N=12 the entanglement entropy peaks near the KT line, but the ordering wavevector stays locked at π/2, so we call it unresolved.

**5. Noise: what shifts and by how much (60 s).** At p=0.01 the order drops to about 60 % of its clean value, but not evenly: the ferromagnet falls to 0.37 while the antiphase keeps 0.59. With fixed thresholds the ferromagnet vanishes from the map. Wherever a threshold crossing survives, it moves to lower h. The field where the order changes fastest barely moves (median shift 0.005). At p=0.05 the purity is only 20 % above that of a fully mixed state. The noise does not change the Hamiltonian; what degrades is the prepared state.

**6. Why (45 s).** We calculated the exact first-order effect of each of the 128 channels separately. The ferromagnet is about 2.3 times more sensitive than simple end-of-circuit counting would predict: errors made early are carried through the later gates, and early layers matter most. The first-order formula gets the direction of the shift right but underestimates its size, because p=0.01 is already nonlinear for this circuit depth. This ranking belongs to this circuit and diagnostic; it is not a law about the phases.

**7. Depth, re-optimisation, second classifier (50 s).** We reran the same experiment with shallower circuits. Shallow circuits are wrong even without noise, and deep ones collect more noise, so the best depth falls as p rises. The antiphase is already accurate with two layers, while the ferromagnet needs all four. That is a second reason the ferromagnet suffers most. We also tried re-optimising the parameters at each noise level: the noisy energy improves, but only by pushing the state towards the field direction; the order parameters barely change, so the state that minimises the noisy energy is not the one that best reveals the phase. A label-free k-means clustering of the same observables reproduces our clean labels at 99 %.

**8. Mitigation (45 s).** Zero-noise extrapolation on the correlators, never on the labels, with the clean result held out: at p=0.01 the correlator error falls six-fold and 98 % of labels are recovered, at the price of about 19 times more variance. At p=0.05 it fails, because no signal is left to extrapolate. Parity verification helps only a little, because most errors keep the parity.

**9. Take-aways (30 s).** An accurate clean diagram; the ferro side converges quickly while the antiphase side drifts towards KT. For a circuit deep enough to be right, 1 % noise already removes absolute order, and 5 % leaves nothing: depth versus noise is the real trade-off. We also ran a quench from the fully polarised state: the Loschmidt rate peaks sharply only for quenches deep into the ordered side, but at p=0.01 the channel error is about eight times the Trotter error, so the dynamical signature does not survive noise. Limitations: small N, approximate overlays, the fixed-parameter protocol, and no shot noise.
